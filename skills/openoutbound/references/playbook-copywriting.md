# Copywriting playbook

- First-touch cold emails are 50-90 words of plain text with one idea, one low-friction interest question, no links or images, and a 2-5 word subject.
- Personalization must come from a real signal or observation with a source URL and date; no fake familiarity, no flattery, no invented numbers.
- The checker scores every draft on grounding, accuracy, tone, length, spam, CTA and personalization; hard failures get one revision, then go to a human.

## 1. Rules for 2026

First touch:
1. Length: 50-90 words in the body (`max_words` 90). Follow-ups: 25-70 words.
2. One idea: one signal, one problem, one offer, one proof point.
3. One call to action: a low-friction interest question ("Worth a look?", "Want the checklist?"). Not a meeting demand, not a calendar link.
4. Plain text: no HTML styling, no images, no attachments, no links in the first touch. Follow-ups may carry one link only if it is a knowledge-base resource the prospect would expect.
5. Subject: 2-5 words, mostly lowercase, specific to them ("crm cleanup before the hire"). No clickbait, no "Re:" or "Fwd:" on a new thread, no emojis, no ALL CAPS.
6. Personalization tied to a real signal or observation with evidence (URL and date in the message `why`), relevant to the offer in the next sentence.
7. No fake familiarity: never imply a meeting, call, conversation or relationship that did not happen.
8. No flattery: state facts, not compliments ("saw your post on X", never "loved your insightful post").
9. Claims about us only from the knowledge base; numbers only if a proof item states them.
10. Peer voice: write like a competent person emailing another, not a brochure. Short sentences, contractions allowed, no exclamation marks.
11. Language: the prospect's language (`writing.language`, then the lead's `language`, then the workspace default). Use formal address where it is the norm (German "Sie", French "vous").
12. The engine appends the compliance footer (sender identity, postal address, unsubscribe line). Never write your own unsubscribe text or remove the footer.

## 2. Why these rules

Figures are vendor data from each vendor's own platform unless marked otherwise; no independent controlled studies exist for most of them. They agree on direction more than on exact numbers.

| Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 50-90 words | Best campaigns under 80 words ([Instantly 2026 benchmark](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026), 2025 data); 50-100 words best and replies "drop sharply" past 100 ([Gong, Jan 2026](https://www.gong.io/blog/do-execs-really-reply-to-cold-email-here-s-what-the-data-says)); 61-80 words among the top bands, though 181-200 also did well ([Hunter 2026](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email), 31M emails). Snov.io saw longer emails do fine, so relevance matters more than a word count. |
| One idea, no pitch | Pitching (talking about your solution, buzzwords, AI, ROI language) cut replies by up to 57% across 28M+ emails ([Gong, 2025](https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says)); 65% of decision makers say cold emails fail for being too sales-focused (Hunter 2026 survey). |
| Interest CTA | The interest CTA performed best for cold email in 304,174 emails; asking for a specific time works at the deal stage, not the cold stage ([Gong Labs, 2020](https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings)). Executives answer CTAs that offer something of value rather than a meeting (Gong, Jan 2026). |
| Subject 2-5 words | Replies were strongest for 3-4 word subjects, and 2-word subjects opened best ([Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/cold-email-benchmarks/), 2025 data); Hunter found 5-6 words best and "quick question" below average (Hunter 2026). Casing made little difference in 5.5M emails ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics), 2024 data): lowercase is a style that reads like internal mail, not a proven lift. |
| No links, no tracking | Campaigns without open tracking replied 7.4% vs 4.4% with it (Hunter 2026); campaigns with link tracking replied far less than those without ([Snov.io](https://snov.io/blog/cold-email-statistics/)). Correlations, not controlled tests, and no study isolates one plain link, so zero links is the safe default. |
| Evidence-based personalization | Two personalized attributes in the body: 5.6% replies vs 3.6% with none; manually edited emails did 18% better than fully automated ones (Hunter 2026). A company-specific topic tripled replies from directors and above ([Gong, 2023](https://www.gong.io/blog/4-data-backed-ways-to-increase-your-email-reply-rate-and-book-that-meeting)). Common signals are becoming commodity; context is what makes them land ([Lavender](https://lavender.ai/blog/signal-based-outbound-a-story-of-missing-context-and-commodity-messaging), opinion). |
| Human in the loop | 69% of US decision makers say AI-written email bothers them (Hunter 2026 survey). In a 12,000-lead split test, AI drafts edited by humans got 7.3% positive replies vs 1.4% for AI-only and 4.2% for human-only ([Saleshandy, Jun 2026](https://www.saleshandy.com/blog/ai-vs-human-cold-emails/); groups used separate domains). |
| No fake "Re:", no deception | Google's sender guidelines: no subject starting with "Re:" or "Fwd:" unless the message is a real reply or forward ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). CAN-SPAM requires subject lines that reflect the content, covers B2B email, and allows penalties up to $53,088 per email ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). |
| Phrase list is about tone | HubSpot, Mailmeteor and ActiveCampaign all say modern filters do not flag single words; reputation, authentication and engagement dominate ([Mailgun](https://www.mailgun.com/blog/deliverability/mailbox-spam-filters/)). The list exists because hype and cliches lower replies and read as automated. |

Benchmarks to judge results against: average reply rate 3.43%, top quartile 5.5%+ (Instantly 2026); median 1.5% ([Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/cold-email-benchmarks/)); 4.5% average, 3% for sales outreach (Hunter 2026). Definitions differ (all replies vs human replies), so compare a campaign with your own history first.

## 3. Structures

Default: Signal -> Relevance -> Value -> CTA. One or two sentences each.

| Part | Job | Example fragment |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | The observed fact, dated or sourced, in plain words | "Your RevOps Manager post says the first project is cleaning up HubSpot duplicates." |
| Relevance | What that usually means for someone in their role, framed as typical, not presumed | "That cleanup usually eats a new hire's first quarter." |
| Value | What we do about it, plus one proof item from the knowledge base | "Tidyrow merges duplicates inside HubSpot on a schedule; Kestrel Labs went from 18% to 2% in three weeks." |
| CTA | One interest question | "Worth a look before your hire starts?" |

Variants:
- Observation -> Consequence -> Offer -> Question: for local businesses and tier C, where the hook is something visible on their site or listing rather than an event.
- Peer proof: "Teams like [similar segment] ran into [problem]; [customer] fixed it by [what]; result [number]. Is [problem] on your list?" Use when there is no fresh signal (tier B and C).
- Resource offer: the CTA offers a specific useful thing ("the five checks new sales leaders run") instead of a call. Good for senior buyers and follow-ups.
- Event: reference the session, booth or topic with the event page as evidence, never a meeting that did not happen.

Subject line patterns: `[their topic]` ("forecast calls"), `[topic] at [company]` ("missed calls at maple grove"), `[their project] + [angle]` ("support for the germany launch"). Keep it something they could have written themselves.

Signature: name, title, company, nothing else. No logos, banners, social icons or quotes. The engine adds the compliance footer below it.

## 4. Personalization

Four tests every personalized line must pass:
1. Evidence: it comes from a signal or research fact with a URL and date, recorded in `why`.
2. Swap test: replace the company name with a competitor's. If the sentence is still true, it is not personalization.
3. Relevance: the next sentence connects it to the offer. Personalization that leads nowhere is decoration.
4. Comfort: it reads like something a thoughtful peer would notice, not like surveillance. No tracked website visits, no personal life, no family, no health, no location of an individual.

Depth by tier (see [playbook-icp.md](playbook-icp.md)): tier A gets a custom opener from the research brief; tier B a signal or segment-level hook; tier C a segment template plus one verified observation.

Freshness (see [playbook-signals.md](playbook-signals.md)): "recently" only for events under 30 days old and within one half-life; name the month for older events; never use a signal older than two half-lives as the hook.

Missing data: if a required slot cannot be filled from evidence, the campaign's `missing_data` policy applies (skip the step or the lead). Never fill a slot with a guess.

## 5. Follow-ups

Each follow-up must add something new. "Just bumping this" adds nothing and trains the reader to ignore you.

Evidence (vendor data): step-2 emails that read like a short reply beat formal follow-ups by about 30%, and each touch should add new value ([Instantly 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)); "never heard back" raised replies but cut meetings booked by 14% ([Gong, 2021](https://www.gong.io/blog/7-tips-for-writing-the-perfect-follow-up-sales-email-according-to-science)); asking for a referral was the most effective follow-up type ([Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)).

Angle ladder for a 4-email sequence:
1. Signal-led problem (first touch).
2. Proof story: how a similar company handled the same problem, with one number from the knowledge base.
3. Useful resource or insight: offer a checklist, benchmark or teardown (offer to send it; attach nothing).
4. Close the loop: a permission question about timing or the right person.

Rules:
- Steps 2 and 3 reply in the same thread (`mode: reply`), so "Re:" is honest there. Step 4 may start a new thread with a new subject if the angle changes.
- Refer back to the earlier email in five words or fewer ("One more angle on the hire").
- Shorter than the first touch: 25-70 words.
- Never guilt ("I'll assume you're not interested"), fake deadlines ("closing your file"), or sarcasm.
- If a new signal appears mid-sequence, it may replace the next angle; the checker still applies freshness rules.
- LinkedIn touches follow the same rules; see [playbook-linkedin.md](playbook-linkedin.md) for length limits.

Pattern checks that are not phrases (deterministic, same severity):
- `Re:` or `Fwd:` in the subject of a `new_thread` email.
- Any link, image or attachment in a first touch; more than one link in a follow-up.
- Exclamation marks; more than one question mark; ALL CAPS words longer than 3 letters (acronyms from the knowledge base excepted).
- Unresolved variables (`{{`, `}}`, `[[`, `]]`) or placeholder text such as `[Company]`.
- Percentages, multiples or money amounts not present in a knowledge-base proof item.
- The long dash character (Unicode U+2014): readers see it as a sign of machine-written text; use a comma, colon or hyphen.
- Emojis, and more than 2 lines of signature above the engine footer.

## 7. Checker rubric

The writing pipeline is: context -> angle -> draft -> deterministic checks + checker model -> one revision -> result. Each dimension is pass or fail; the model also gives a 1-5 quality score for personalization and relevance, used in A/B analysis.

| Dimension | Pass | Hard fail | How checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grounding | Every claim about the prospect maps to a research fact or signal with URL and date; every claim about us maps to a knowledge item; ids recorded in `why` | Any unsupported claim, invented number, customer or result | Model, plus `why` ids must exist |
| Accuracy | Names, company, title, dates and numbers match stored records; signal within freshness rules | Wrong name or company; stale signal called recent; wrong language or form of address | Deterministic fields, model for meaning |
| Tone | Peer-to-peer, plain, calm; matches voice samples and `tone_notes` | Hype, flattery, guilt, fake urgency, fake familiarity, sarcasm | Phrase list plus model |
| Length | Body within limits (first touch 50-90 words, follow-up 25-70); subject 2-5 words | Over `max_words`; first touch under 35 words; subject over 5 words, or an AI-written subject under 2 | Deterministic |
| Spam | No listed phrase; pattern checks pass | Any hit in a first touch | Deterministic |
| CTA | Exactly one ask, low friction, answerable in one line | No ask, two or more asks, a meeting demand or calendar link in a first touch | Model plus question-mark count |
| Personalization | Passes evidence, swap, relevance and comfort tests | Generic, flattering, irrelevant to the offer, sensitive or surveillance-like | Model |

Outcomes:
- All pass: the draft follows the campaign `review_level` (`every`, `first` or `unsure`).
- Any hard fail: one revision with the failure reasons as instructions.
- Still failing after revision: `pending_review` with reasons, or skip per `missing_data` if the cause is missing evidence. Never send a draft that failed grounding or accuracy.
- Under `review_level: unsure`, any draft with a personalization or relevance score below 3 goes to review.

Teaching: when a human edits a draft, the engine proposes a campaign rule from the edit ("do not mention funding amounts"). Accept rules that generalize; reject one-offs.

## 8. Before and after

All companies and people are invented; domains use example.com. Proof numbers come from invented knowledge-base items.

1. Hiring signal (Tidyrow, CRM data cleanup, to a software company)
- Before (58 words): "Hi Jana, I hope this email finds you well! I came across Quillfeather Analytics and was really impressed by your amazing growth. At Tidyrow, we offer a cutting-edge, AI-powered platform that helps companies like yours supercharge their CRM. Our customers see 300% ROI! Would you have 30 minutes this week for a quick call? Here's my calendar: https://cal.example.com/sam"
- Problems: filler opener, flattery, hype phrases, unsupported 300%, meeting demand, calendar link.
- After, subject "crm cleanup before the hire" (66 words): "Hi Jana, your RevOps Manager post says the hire's first project is cleaning up duplicate and stale HubSpot records. That cleanup usually eats a new hire's whole first quarter. Tidyrow finds and merges duplicates inside HubSpot on a schedule; Kestrel Labs went from 18% duplicates to 2% in three weeks. Worth a look before your new hire starts, so they can begin on pipeline work instead?"
- Why it works: signal with source (job post URL, date), relevance framed as typical, one proof item, one interest question.

2. Funding round (Polyglot Pods, multilingual support, to a logistics company)
- Before (43 words): "Congrats on the Series B!! Exciting times at Larkspur Freight. We help fast-growing companies scale their customer support with world-class, seamless multilingual solutions. I'd love to hop on a quick call to discuss synergies. Are you the right person? If not, who is?"
- Problems: bare congratulations, hype, two questions, vague value, meeting ask.
- After, subject "support for the germany launch" (63 words): "Hi Tomas, the Series B announcement says the money goes into launching Germany and the Netherlands this year. Support in German and Dutch is usually what lags behind a launch like that. Polyglot Pods runs native-speaking support teams that go live in about three weeks; Fernway used us for their Benelux launch. Would it help to see how they staffed the first month?"
- Why it works: uses the stated use of funds, not the round itself.

3. Local clinic, review pattern (RingRight, call answering, to a dental practice)
- Before (30 words): "Dear Sir/Madam, Are you losing patients?? Our revolutionary AI receptionist is 100% guaranteed to double your bookings! Click here for a FREE trial: https://ringright.example.com/free. Don't miss out, limited spots available!"
- Problems: generic greeting, fear hook, spam phrases, caps, link, guarantee.
- After, subject "missed calls at maple grove" (68 words): "Hi Dr. Anand, several recent Google reviews for Maple Grove Dental mention trouble getting through by phone, mostly around lunchtime. For a practice with three dentists, those are often new patients calling. RingRight answers overflow calls in your practice's name and books straight into your schedule. A two-location clinic in Riverton set it up in a week. Would a short summary of how they did it be useful?"
- Why it works: a business-level pattern from public reviews (listing URL as evidence), no patient details, local proof, resource CTA.

4. New executive (Tidyrow, to a new VP Sales)
- Before (45 words): "Hi Marcus, congrats on the new role! I know you're busy, so I'll keep this short. Tidyrow is the leading CRM data platform trusted by hundreds of revenue teams. I'd love to pick your brain about your plans. Do you have 15 minutes on Thursday?"
- Problems: generic congratulations, filler, unsupported "leading" and "hundreds", meeting ask.
- After, subject "first 60 days at corvid" (57 words): "Hi Marcus, Corvid Parcel's announcement says you now lead sales, with a push into mid-market shippers. New sales leaders often audit the pipeline in their first 60 days, and stalled deals with no next step are the usual finding. Tidyrow flags those in HubSpot every morning. Want the five checks our customers run in their first week?"

5. Follow-up (step 2 of example 1)
- Before (24 words): "Hi Jana, just bumping this to the top of your inbox. Did you get a chance to read my last email? Let me know!"
- After, same thread (48 words): "Hi Jana, one more angle on the RevOps hire. When Kestrel Labs hired their first RevOps lead, her first month went to dedupe work, so they ran Tidyrow in parallel and she started on forecasting instead. Is CRM cleanup already covered in the onboarding plan, or still open?"
- Why it works: a new angle (proof story), a short back-reference, a question that is easy to answer.

6. Event (Tidyrow, to a panel speaker they never met)
- Before (26 words): "Hi Priya, great meeting you at RevOps Days! As discussed, I'm sending over some info about Tidyrow. Let me know when you're free for a demo."
- Problems: fake meeting, fake "as discussed", no value, meeting ask.
- After, subject "forecast calls" (53 words): "Hi Priya, your RevOps Days Lisbon panel covered forecast calls that turn into arguments about data. One thing that helps teams we work with: an automatic list of deals without a next step, sent the morning before the call. I can send you the template we share with customers. Would that be useful?"
- Why it works: the event agenda page is the evidence; the offer is a resource, not a demo.
